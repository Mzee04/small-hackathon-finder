Read this before picking one – honest limits Honest limits: this list covers Devpost plus DEV challenges, Superteam Earn bounties open globally, and online events from MLH. DoraHacks publishes a hackathon API, but that site often answers this tool with a human-verification page; when it does, DoraHacks is skipped and the other sources are still listed. A contest is left out when its listing says it is students-only or charges an entry fee. Superteam listings that are limited to one region are left out. USD-pegged stablecoins (USDC, USDT, USDG, DAI, PYUSD) are counted as one US dollar each, the same way the hard-coded rates treat cash; other crypto tokens stay unconverted and sort after every converted prize. Registration counts are not submissions - most registered teams never submit, so real odds are usually better than the numbers suggest, but the ratio is only a heuristic, not a prediction. Prize amounts are self-reported by organisers and are not verified, and may be split across many tracks or teams. Amounts are shown as listed and converted to approximate US dollars with hard-coded exchange rates (taken 2026-10-26, not live); that dollar figure is used for ranking and shown next to non-dollar prizes. Listings flagged 'verify currency' look like rupees mislabeled as dollars (Indian-style digit grouping such as 1,55,000, or a very large dollar prize at a college or university with very few registrants). Those rows are ranked below the others, using a conservative rupee value. A currency with no known rate is listed after every converted prize. Prize text that mentions credits, bitcoin, crypto, tokens, subscriptions, swag, or goodies is marked 'includes non-cash prizes'. The countdown uses the submission deadline. Devpost only gives a date, so that deadline is the last second of the UTC day; a feed that gives a clock time uses that time. A row disappears once the moment has passed. Always read each hackathon's rules page for eligibility, team-size limits and exact prize conditions before entering. Who can participate is read from each hackathon page. Listings limited to students, high school, professionals, partners, or a required team are hidden until you ask to see them. A country list that is tighter than Devpost's usual sanctions exceptions is marked country-limited, and a page that clearly requires a physical robot or other hardware is marked too. Neither of those hides the row. If that sidebar cannot be read, the row stays visible with no eligibility badge. Those sidebar rules are for Devpost. Other sources use the fields their own feed provides.
