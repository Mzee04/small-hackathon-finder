Read this before picking one – honest limits Honest limits: this list only covers hackathons listed on Devpost. Registration counts are not submissions - most registered teams never submit, so real odds are usually better than the numbers suggest, but the ratio is only a heuristic, not a prediction. Prize amounts are self-reported by organisers and are not verified, and may be split across many tracks or teams. Amounts are shown as listed and converted to approximate US dollars with hard-coded exchange rates (taken 2026-10-26, not live); that dollar figure is used for ranking and shown next to non-dollar prizes. Listings flagged 'verify currency' look like rupees mislabeled as dollars (Indian-style digit grouping such as 1,55,000, or a very large dollar prize at a college or university with very few registrants). Those rows are ranked below the others, using a conservative rupee value. A currency with no known rate is listed after every converted prize. Prize text that mentions credits, bitcoin, crypto, tokens, subscriptions, swag, or goodies is marked 'includes non-cash prizes'. The countdown uses the submission end date (through the end of that UTC day), and a row disappears once that moment has passed. Always read each hackathon's rules page for eligibility, team-size limits and exact prize conditions before entering.
